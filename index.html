Abstract

Diffusion-based audio-visual speech enhancement (AVSE) iteratively recovers speech from noisy audio conditioned on temporally aligned visual cues, yet real-world deployment may introduce visual degradation and audio-visual mismatch that misguide the reverse diffusion process. Our analysis indicates that the effect of visual conditioning depends on both the pretrained model and the acoustic input, making visual quality alone an incomplete indicator of its reliability. Motivated by these findings, we propose Reliability-Guided Inference (RGI), a framework that scores the reliability of visual conditioning from internal visual representations and model responses and regulates its contribution during reverse diffusion. Specifically, at each reverse step, RGI weights the difference between audio-visual and audio-only predictions according to the reliability estimate, without updating the pretrained models. To retain visual cues that can remain useful at low input signal-to-noise ratio, RGI imposes a lower bound on the conditioning coefficient, the conditioning floor. A diffusion-step schedule further modulates the retained visual contribution along the sampling trajectory. Experiments across four corpora and three frozen diffusion AVSE backbones show that RGI improves enhancement under a range of visual distribution shifts, with gains that depend on the backbone and corpus. Further evaluation using recordings collected with the Ameca humanoid robot demonstrates its effectiveness under real-world audio-visual conditions.
