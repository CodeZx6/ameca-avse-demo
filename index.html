Setup

A laptop beside the Ameca humanoid plays the noisy input of each scene while its screen shows the video the enhancement model receives. Ameca hears the noisy mixture through its microphone, pauses for a short fixed interval, then voices the enhanced speech through its speaker with lip motion driven from the enhanced audio itself: the visemes are laid over the voiced segments found in the output waveform, so the mouth stays closed while the input plays and opens only on the enhanced speech. Each robot video shows the visual input in the corner while Ameca is listening. Two arms answer the same scenes: the frozen audio-visual diffusion backbone (AV-UDiffSE, backbone (AV)) and the same frozen backbone with the paper’s reliability-guided inference (backbone + RGI); nothing is retrained and the checkpoint is shared.

Visual conditions applied to the mouth crop the model sees (audio is untouched):

  • cleanunperturbed mouth crop (the reference visual condition)
  • occlusionpart of the mouth region is covered in the input video
  • blackoutframes of the input video are blacked out
  • wrong speakerthe video belongs to a different speaker (GRID: any sentence from another speaker)
  • freezethe video freezes on a frame while the audio continues
  • avoffsetthe video is shifted in time relative to the audio
  • lowresthe mouth crop is heavily down-sampled

Method. RGI = reliability-guided inference: a zero-training, inference-time scheme that gauges how much the visual stream can be trusted and steers the frozen audio-visual diffusion sampler accordingly, so a corrupted mouth crop stops dragging the enhanced speech away from the audio evidence. The backbone is AV-UDiffSE in every clip; AO (audio only) and CADS are shown in the tables for context only. Credits. Anonymous authors. Ameca is a humanoid robot by Engineered Arts. TCD-TIMIT and GRID utterances mixed with DEMAND noise. Disclaimer. The SI-SDR and WER values on this page are per-clip illustrations for the played examples; they are not the paper’s table numbers, which are averages over the full test sets. The pause between hearing and answering is a fixed constant, not a measured inference latency.
